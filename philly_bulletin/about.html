How it works · Philadelphia

Make real life easier than the feed.

A city is full of ways to become a regular somewhere. The Bulletin makes those chances as easy to find as the things already competing for your attention.

Every event we can find in Philadelphia.

Not just the ones with a marketing budget. The reading series in the back of the bookshop, the Tuesday night run club, the block cleanup, the free lecture at the library — the things that never make a listings page because nobody paid to put them there.

The Bulletin goes looking for them. It re-checks the city's calendars regularly and prints what it finds.

Scored for community, not hype.

An AI reads every listing and scores it out of ten. Not for how big it is or what it costs — for whether showing up might connect you to somebody.

Every score comes with its reason, written about that particular event. Open any listing and you'll see all six. The reasons are the point. Read them and disagree freely: they're an argument, not a verdict.

Six questions, asked of every listing
  • Social quality

    Will people talk to each other, or sit in rows facing forward?

  • Serendipity

    Might you meet somebody you had no reason to meet?

  • Solo friendliness

    Can you show up alone without it being strange?

  • Community building

    Does anything survive the night — a group, a habit, a neighbor?

  • Uniqueness

    Could you get this anywhere else, or only here?

  • Value for price

    Is it worth what it costs? Free counts for a lot.

↻ means it happens every week —
going twice builds something.

Anything marked ↻ comes back: every week, every other week, or every month. A limited run doesn't count, because a run isn't a habit. Both the bulletin and the map can show you only these.

A one-off concert is an evening. A thing that happens every Tuesday is how you end up with people who notice when you don't come. Go once and you've been out. Go twice and you're a regular. That's the whole bet.

How it works
  1. 01Find. Every calendar the Bulletin can get at — venues, libraries, colleges, ticket sites, neighborhood groups. A few places got on the list from a flyer on a telephone pole. All of it checked again on a regular schedule.
  2. 02Sort. Repeat sightings from the same calendar are collapsed, addresses are placed on the map, and anything that comes back week after week is recognized as one recurring thing. Two different calendars can still occasionally print the same event twice.
  3. 03Read. An AI reads each listing against the six questions above and writes a score and a reason for each. Something that repeats is read once, and every date carries that reading.
  4. 04Print. Best first, grouped by day. Nothing is promoted and nothing is buried — a listing nobody has read yet still appears, it simply has no score.
  5. 05Map. The same listings, by neighborhood. Bigger pin, better night. Pick a neighborhood to read only that one.
What it isn't
  1. —Not a ticket seller. Nothing is sold here. Every listing links out to whoever is actually running it.
  2. —Not promotion. Nobody pays to be listed and nobody can. There is no advertising here, and no way to buy a better score.
  3. —Not complete. The coverage is honest but partial. A quiet neighborhood on the map usually means the Bulletin hasn't found its calendars yet, not that nothing happens there. The ledger, linked at the foot of every page, shows what has been found so far.
  4. —Not the venue. Times, prices and cancellations come from whoever is holding the event. Check with them before you go.
